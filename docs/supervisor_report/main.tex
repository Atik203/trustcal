\textit{On the title.} The registered FYDP title is ``Trust-Calibrated Multi-Agent Scientific Deliberation for Mitigating Sycophantic Consensus in LLM Reasoning'' and stays on the FYDP cover until a change is approved. The paper title above is used here because the registered title promises a mitigation (``for Mitigating'') the experiments have not yet measured. The paper's defensible contribution is narrower: it formalises \emph{when} evidence-grounded trust helps and when it amplifies a wrong majority, builds a controlled benchmark for the failure, and tests the sign reversal.

\section*{1. Project update}

Three heterogeneous LLMs answer the same scientific multiple-choice question, read one another's reasoning over three rounds, and revise. The treatment arm (B8) aggregates the final answer with trust weights computed from how well each agent's claims check out against retrieved external evidence (PubMed, arXiv, Semantic Scholar); the control arm (B8-no-trust) runs identical prompts, evidence, and source partitioning but aggregates by majority vote. The failure under study is \emph{sycophantic consensus}: a confidently wrong majority pressures a correct minority agent into agreeing, after which the vote records the wrong answer as consensus. The confirmatory endpoints (accuracy and Correct-Minority Retention on pressure-eligible items) are frozen in the pre-registration; no result below claims them.

Two findings came out of the smoke itself. First, one injection item showed a \emph{transient} effect the check initially missed: the targeted agent moved D\,$\to$\,C under the injected consensus in round 2, then reverted to D by round 3. The smoke check compared only round 1 with round 3, so it reported a failure on a real delivered effect; the check now detects any post-injection change, with a regression test, and injection passes 5/5. Second, the oracle arm's fifth record was lost to a spot eviction, not to the pipeline; the 4 completed records are all valid and all final answers correct.

\textit{Plain reading:} at $n=25$ per arm the confidence intervals overlap almost completely and the two-observation gap (16 vs.\ 14) is noise; CCR/CMR endpoints are mostly not applicable with no pressure arms in the prefix. The trust machinery is observably present in B8 (19 trajectories, AUROC/Brier computable) and absent in the control (majority finals 25/25), exactly as designed. This prefix demonstrates feasibility (rows run, resume, and time correctly) and gives timing data --- it is \textbf{not} an efficacy result and is not reported as one.
